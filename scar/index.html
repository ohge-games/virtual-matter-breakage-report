Engine release/iteration073 (384c388, built 2026-10-05), native client 73.0.1554, CLI 0.6.5. Found while building SCAR, a destruction FPS whose core loop is persistent terrain edits. Same 5W format as the Ashlight report (v33, https://ohge-games.github.io/virtual-matter-breakage-report/): Who / What / When / Where / Why, then impact, repro, mitigation and suggested fix. The 5Ws, per breakage

F1

Static edits reach connected clients through a slow, capped stream

CriticalWhy: measured↑ index
Who
every player in a world whose terrain changes during play - the whole premise of a destruction FPS.
What
server-side static edits arrive on a connected client 1-4.5 s later when the stream is idle (6 single 0.8 m removes: 0.9, 4.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.1, 1.3 s). Under load they queue: a fresh client that is still streaming the map received a single 0.8 m remove somewhere between 30 s and 3 minutes later. After bursts (40 removes at 5/s in one 8 m cell) the client was still wrong at 12-32% of sample points 75 s later, and an 18-crater burst (r 2.5 m) never arrived at all on a client that stayed connected for 15+ minutes (the client showed the cell flat; the server had craters to the pad floor). A client that connected later saw everything at once.
When
whenever edits are made while clients are connected; worst for bursts and large edits, and while a client is still loading detail (a fresh client was still pulling 68-107 KB/s eight minutes after joining a heavily edited 48 x 32 m pad).
Where
static voxel streaming (_AmStreaming). Server:GetStreamingStats() shows each client in a STALLING lag state with stallingSendBPS = 500000; the client receives 24-110 KB/s. A paint over the stuck cell, an LOD bubble, moving the camera 600 m away and back, and AE:ReloadGeometry() did not resync it.
Why
edits are not pushed as small deltas with priority; they wait behind (or are coalesced into) bulk chunk streaming that is rate-capped per client. Under a burst, intermediate versions appear to be dropped and the final version is not always re-sent.

Impact

what a player sees and collides with can differ from the server for seconds to minutes after any dig or blast. Collision divergence drops players into holes that aren't there (or holds them on ground that is gone), and cover differs per player.

Repro

bench/run_bench.py (A1-A4 configs: 40 removes at 5/s, r 0.5-2.5 m, one cell, client camera 9 m above). bench/lat1.py <client> x z for one edit.

Mitigation shipped in SCAR


• every gameplay edit goes through one scheduler: 4 ops/s global, a 6-op burst, 0.8 s spacing per 8 m cell, a 4 s settle window between remove and add, a 24-op queue cap, and 2 ops per frame at most;
• digs are single capsule strokes every 0.9 s (not continuous), and craters are single spheres;
• bullets never edit terrain;
• a divergence detector probes clients every 5 s at recently edited spots and under their feet, teleports a player standing on ground the server doesn't have, and tightens the budget while divergence is open;
• hit occlusion is decided on the server's terrain only.

Suggested fix

version each static chunk and push edits as small high-priority deltas; re-send any chunk whose version a client hasn't acknowledged; expose a per-client "resync region" call and a per-edit delivery callback or acknowledgement.

F2

A connected client can stop receiving static edits entirely

CriticalWhy: observed↑ index
Who
any connected client after a burst of terrain edits.
What
native client #1 received none of 40 removes made at 5/s, nor any later edit (3 pillars of 4 m concrete, several holes) for 15+ minutes. Client #4 showed the same after an 18-op burst. The server-side screenshot client showed every edit.
When
after edit bursts while connected; seen 3 times in one session.
Where
static streaming to that client. Reconnecting fixed it every time.
Why
unknown; consistent with the flow controller (senderRecvLagDeviation pinned at 16383, finalRatioPct = 0) never leaving its stalled state for that client.

Impact

a player in that state plays on a different map for the rest of the session.

Mitigation

the edit budget (F1); the divergence detector counts divergences older than 90 s as unrepaired and tells the player to rejoin (F5 key in the browser).

Suggested fix

recover the flow state, or force a resync of dirty chunks, when a client's streaming has been stalled for N seconds.

F3

Clients drop when the server stalls for ~15 s or more

HighWhy: measured↑ index
Who
anyone connected or connecting while the server saves its terrain.
What
a native client that connected just as SCAR's empty-server maintenance ran (reclaim, then a 17.3 s terrain save) was disconnected and did not reconnect. Its log shows "New max ui client message sending time: 11057 ms", then "Connection to server lost".
When
during any main-thread stall longer than roughly 10-15 s (terrain save B19).
Where
client/server TCP keepalive.

Impact

the empty-server save window is not safe for players who arrive during it, and the native client offers no reconnect.

Mitigation

maintenance now checks for connected AND pending clients on every step and abandons the reclaim/save when anyone arrives. The traversal check that took one frame (7,569 rays) now runs as one row per frame.

Suggested fix

async terrain save (B19); a longer timeout during a known save; automatic reconnect in the native client.

F4

Native client loses its connection with no auto-reconnect

HighWhy: observed↑ index
Who
a native client in a long session.
What
two disconnects not caused by a server stall: 16:40:03 (after "New max ui client message sending time: 29600 ms") and 17:00:06 ("TcpClient::SendMsg: failed to send a message to server" with no preceding gap in the client log; the client was at 88 fps). The window stays open, showing a frozen scene; nothing reconnects.
When
6 and 17 minutes into play. Update (8 occurrences; the 8th at 19:00 after a 51.8 s stall, with no screenshot involved): the client log shows "New max ui client message sending time" climbing to 29.6 s, 51.8 s, 58 s and 253.6 s before "Connection to server lost"; the server then reports the client gone. The client keeps rendering at 88-100 fps throughout, so it is not frozen; its upstream channel is blocked. Three of the seven followed a screenshot --client capture within seconds to minutes; the others came while the client was streaming terrain heavily (150-216 KB/s downstream, footprint 346 MB and growing) during edit benches. A hidden (throttled) browser tab also fell behind and its divergence probes failed.
Where
TcpClient.cpp:197/317; Client.cpp:2654 ("ui client message sending time").

Impact

long tests and long play sessions end silently; the server keeps the player for a while (B49 ghost).

Mitigation

none possible in game code beyond tolerating rejoin (SCAR restores the player's team, class and score by name).

Suggested fix

automatic reconnect with session resume; surface the disconnect reason.

F5

Client ray and collision data is LOD-quantised a few metres from the camera

HighWhy: measured↑ index
What
over an untouched flat pad, server and client rays agree exactly; over dug ground 4-11 m from the client camera, client ray heights are higher than the server's in steps of 0.125 m (and some 0.25 m), even with an LOD priority bubble around the area.

Impact

any client-side check of terrain (movement, divergence probes, impact effects) needs a tolerance of 0.3 m or more; "divergence" measured with tighter tolerances is mostly LOD.

Mitigation

the divergence detector uses 0.6 m; the bench reports both 0.3 m and 0.6 m mismatches.

Suggested fix

an API to read the server's height for a column, or a client query at a forced LOD.

F6

The edit guard's budget is per frame, not per op

MediumWhy: measured↑ index
What
a pad reset issued 4 large ops in one Lua call (remove, add, remove, add; ~491 baseline-m2 each). The 4th threw "frame voxel budget exhausted (~697 already issued this frame...)", leaving half the pad removed and not re-added.

Impact

any batch (map builders, resets, reclaim) must spread ops over frames and handle a mid-batch refusal; a naive loop corrupts terrain.

Mitigation

every SCAR batch runs one op (or 2-4 small ops) per frame and retries "budget" refusals on the next frame.

Suggested fix

an option to queue past the budget (deferred to later frames) instead of throwing.

F7

ForceStatic() throws on the client

MediumWhy: confirmed↑ index

Cast:Ray(...):ForceStatic() and Collide(...):ForceStatic() on a client raise "ForceStatic() only available on server". Client code must use plain rays and filter dynamic objects with Ignore. Suggested fix: allow it on clients (the client has static data) or document it.

F8

CLI 0.6.5 files API requires a precondition on every PUT

Medium↑ index

Uploading a NEW file with no precondition returns HTTP 428. The vm-agent-tools push-only deploy (built against older behaviour) failed for all new files. Fix shipped in vm-agent-tools/cli-ext/vmpush.mjs: createOnly (If-None-Match: *) for new files, etag re-read on conflict.

F9

Re-parenting keeps the world transform

Low↑ index

child.parent = obj keeps the child's world position, so a camera created at the origin and parented to an eye object ended up at the player's feet. Set localPos / localRot after parenting.

F10

Streaming stats read as stalled for healthy clients

Low↑ index

Server:GetStreamingStats() reports senderRecvLagDeviationFlowState = STALLING and finalRatioPct = 0 for a freshly connected client that does receive data. The fields are undocumented, so they cannot be used to detect F2.

F11

A zero-length ray throws

MediumWhy: measured↑ index

Cast:Ray(p, Vec3(0,0,0)):Run() raises "ray is zero length". Inside a util:schedule chain or an Update loop the error stops the chain silently (the B16 bench's sampler died this way when it picked the same player twice). Gameplay code hits it whenever two positions coincide: a grenade exploding exactly at a chest, a bot aiming at a target standing on it, a projectile at rest. SCAR guards every ray whose vector can be zero. Suggested fix: return nil (no hit) for a zero-length ray, as most engines do.

F12

Every static edit costs one 30-50 ms server frame, whatever its size

CriticalWhy: measured↑ index
Who
every player of a world that edits terrain during play.
What
single gameplay edits through SCAR's scheduler on an idle server (17 ms frames): dig capsule r 0.7 -> worst frame 30-33 ms; frag sphere r 1.1 -> 30-34 ms; breach sphere r 1.8 -> 50-52 ms; Bloom (3 small adds) -> 25 ms. A sphere with 3.9x the volume of a dig costs the same frame, so the cost is mostly per op, not per voxel. Each op also adds ~40-110 ms of update work spread over the following frames.
When
every static Vox op (Remove/Add/Paint).
Where
the commit of a static op (the voxel system flush, LOD rebuild and streaming of the touched chunks).

Impact

a p99 frame budget of 33 ms allows about one spike frame per 100 frames (0.6/s). Worst-case stress (8 players firing 214 shots/s with lag compensation costs nothing measurable; terrain at ~3.5 ops/s pushes p99 to 39 ms, max 51 ms). Even 1 op/s gives p99 36 ms. Real 8-player bot matches (fewer edits) held p99 16.8 ms for 5 rounds.

Mitigation shipped

fewer, bigger dig strokes (1.2 s, r 0.8); a global budget of 2.5 ops/s; a frame governor that cuts the edit rate to 40% while more than 1% of the last 300 frames exceed 33 ms; bullets never edit terrain.

Suggested fix

amortise the per-op commit (batch ops per frame into one flush, defer LOD rebuilds), or move it to a worker; expose a per-op cost estimate.

F13

The server-side screenshot client goes stale after a map-scale rebuild

HighWhy: observed↑ index

After SCAR's clean rebuild (clear all static terrain, rebuild 112 x 112 m: 9.3k jobs, then a dressing pass) the CLI's default screenshot (rendered by the server-side client) showed only the southern half of the map, with a floating wireframe of edges over the north, unchanged across 4 captures over several minutes and two camera angles. Server rays (the verify script) found all terrain; a freshly connected native client rendered the whole map correctly. This is B3/B28 at map scale (remove then add everywhere while connected), and it means the agent's main visual check is wrong after any big rebuild. Mitigation: verify with server rays and a freshly connected native client. Suggested fix: resync the server-side client's stream after large edits, or reconnect it per screenshot.

F14

A freshly joined client receives gameplay edits 12-45 s late

CriticalWhy: measured↑ index
Who
every player who joined a world within the last several minutes (in a 4-minute-round game: everyone).
What
bench/gameplay_edit_bench.py on the dressed Scarfield map, native client joined ~2 min earlier, standing 10 m from the edits: single digger (1 stroke/s, one cell) delivered 9/9, latency avg 14.5 s, p90 24 s, max 27 s; the global budget (2.5 ops/s over 6 cells) delivered 6/10 within 60 s (avg 12 s, max 23 s), 4 never arrived; frag craters (1 per 3 s) 8/8, avg 26 s, max 45 s. On the empty off-map pad earlier, a single edit took 1-4.5 s.
When
while the client is still streaming the map: 74 MB received after 8 minutes, 150-216 KB/s, client footprint 323-346 MB and still growing.
Where
static streaming: edits are not prioritised over bulk detail streaming (F1).

Impact

the digger's own hole appears seconds to tens of seconds after the stroke; craters appear long after the blast; collision differs from the server meanwhile.

Mitigation shipped

gameplay never depends on client terrain: exposure, crate pop-up, hit occlusion and validation all use server terrain; the crate surfaces at the hole's rim; dig progress is reported by the server ("CACHE 1.1 m"); client-side dirt/scorch effects play immediately; the divergence detector lifts players out of terrain they are inside of. Lower-resolution static terrain (16 vpm) would quarter the stream, but no such empty terrain asset exists (32/64/128 only).

Suggested fix

push edit deltas ahead of bulk detail streaming; offer a 16 vpm static terrain option.

F15

An object name set after creation never reaches clients

HighWhy: measured↑ index

The SCAR avatar scale bench cloned "Scar Rig 10" 40 times with Scene:CloneObject and renamed each clone ("Scar Bench Rig k") on the server. The server listed 40 bench rigs and one "Scar Rig 10"; the connected native client listed 41 objects named "Scar Rig 10" and none of the new names, minutes later. Client code that finds objects by name therefore never finds a clone. Workaround: pass Object.id (documented as unique across server and clients) and resolve with Scene:GetObjectById. Suggested fix: replicate name changes, or document that names are creation-time only.

F16

Cloning a multi-part voxel rig stalls the server about 110 ms per rig

HighWhy: measured↑ index
What
Scene:CloneObject of a 12-part rig, with each part bound to a baked .am asset, took 96-113 ms of server main thread per rig (about 9 ms per voxel part, max 144 ms). Ten clones in one Lua call stalled the server for about 1.1 s.

After

with 40 extra rigs (480 dynamic voxel objects), the server median frame went from 16.9 to 29.8 ms while the new objects streamed out. Once streaming settled, the median was back to 16.7 ms with all 40 rigs still present (p99 17 ms), so there is no lasting per-object cost.

Why it matters

every clone deep-copies its assets on the main thread (B29). VM reported that B44 engine crashes happen during rig asset copy and free.

Mitigation

SCAR binds each rig part's asset once at pool build, one part per frame, and never rebinds or frees. Bench rigs are deactivated rather than destroyed.

F17

Clients draw terrain as grey placeholders while its collision is correct

CriticalWhy: observed repeatedly↑ index
Who
every player. The playtester reported it several times: "lots of world loading issues while moving; sometimes part of the map isn't generated while turning", then "large chunks of map aren't rendered".
What
whole regions of the 112 m map render as flat grey surfaces with wireframe chunk edges, with floating bushes and boulders. A screenshot pair 3 minutes apart from the same spot showed the east half missing, then the west half missing while the east rendered.
Where
the native client (73.0.1554), after 10-50 minutes of play on a field with ongoing digging. The client sat at 5.8 GB resident at launch and 7.4 GB after 50 minutes (65 GB host), so memory pressure is not the cause.

Collision is fine

client rays over a 6 x 5 grid inside a missing region matched the server's terrain heights within the known LOD offset (F5). The client has the voxel data and is not drawing it.

Mitigation shipped, partial at best

a third LOD priority bubble around the player (Client:AddPlayerLODPriorityBubbleShape, radius 25 m, size 0.6). The SDK controller's bubbles are 2 m and 4 m only. A four-direction test from the player's own camera, with the bubble on, still showed grey untextured placeholder ground in one direction. The bubble does not remove the holes.

Suggested fix

keep a radius around the camera resident regardless of view direction, and expose the render streamer's budget.

F18

Lua resets silently orphan imagine() generations

HighWhy: observed ↑ index
What
generations started with imagine(prompt, Object, opts) never finish if any .lua file is pushed while they run. The object keeps its Gen Progress script indefinitely (observed 25+ minutes) and no error is printed. Four weapon jobs were lost this way, then two more.

Also seen

one carbine job ended with no asset and no visible error. The documented "[imagine] generation failed" line was not seen through the CLI.

Contrast

fast = true, hq = true soldier and weapon jobs finished in 2-6 minutes when nothing was pushed.

Impact

an agent that iterates on scripts while models generate loses the models.

Mitigation

no .lua pushes while jobs are pending, and staging below the map (staging in the air left models visible in players' sky).

Suggested fix

resume orphaned jobs after a Lua reset (the docs say the Shape Manager does this; it did not here), or fail them visibly.

F19

Small per-frame Lua garbage produces 10-14 ms client GC spikes

MediumWhy: measured↑ index

SCAR's procedural avatars allocated about 20 small tables per rig per pose (7 rigs at 60 fps, about 7k tables/s). The avatar script measured 87 of 1,015 frames over 2 ms with 14 ms peaks, although its own work averages 0.3 ms. Reusing the tables gave 2 of 1,744 frames over 2 ms with a 2.4 ms peak. The spikes are incremental GC steps charged to whichever script allocates. Rays (0.08-0.13 ms), localPos/localRot writes (0.0008 ms) and Vec3/Quat construction (0.0003 ms) are cheap. A controlled A/B of client fps against injected garbage was inconclusive (25-36 fps either way, with the window unfocused). Suggested fix: document the GC budget, and expose collectgarbage("step") control or a generational GC on clients.

F20

A scoped zoom still renders distance LOD

LowWhy: observed↑ index

At FOV 14 (4.6x), the Marksman scope shows the same voxel LOD as the unzoomed view, so distant targets and terrain look coarse when magnified. The renderer picks LOD by distance, not projected size. Suggested fix: scale the LOD distance by FOV, or expose an LOD bias for the active camera. ## N1. What worked (for the engine team)

Cross-scale copy

Vox:Copy from an hq generated model at scale 0.179 into scale-1 parts resamples cleanly; a 10-part soldier was cut in 0.4 s.

Rays on scaled objects

plain (non-forced) rays hit scaled dynamic objects, unlike B31's forced rays.

Tint

VoxelData.tintColor team tint and a damage flash replicate and cost nothing measurable.

Procedural animation

client-side animation of 10 flat 12-part rigs (two-bone IK, world-planted feet, verlet ragdolls) costs 0.28 ms/frame. Server cost is unchanged (p99 16.9 ms).

F21

Clients cannot see a streamed object's voxel data

MediumWhy: measured↑ index

On the native client, every rig part (rendered and visible) and every viewmodel part reported VoxelData.data == nil, VoxelData.asset == nil and isLoaded == false, while the server reported the bound asset and the volume. A client therefore cannot tell whether an object's voxels have arrived. SCAR's viewmodel waited for data.volume > 0 and never switched on. Workaround: trust the server's binding. Suggested fix: expose a client-side "streamed / LOD level" flag on VoxelData.

F22

Dynamic objects stream only near their server position

HighWhy: measured↑ index

SCAR poses rigs and viewmodels on the client (receiveTransform = false).

Viewmodel

the server pool parked the viewmodel set 40 m under the map. The owning client never received its voxels, so the guns stayed invisible in front of the camera. Moving the set's server position to 6 m under the owner's feet made it appear within seconds.

Rigs

remote rigs whose server root lagged the player (re-centred every 3 s) were reported as "enemy models sometimes not showing". Re-centring at 4 Hz addressed it. The engine decides streaming and LOD from the server-side transform, not from where the client draws the object. Any client-predicted or client-posed object therefore needs its server position kept near where it is drawn (inside the ground if it must stay hidden). Suggested fix: let a client report where it renders an object it owns, or add a per-object "always stream to its owner" flag.

F23

Streaming to clients causes the server's frame spikes, and a long session makes them worse

CriticalWhy: measured↑ index
Who
every player once the world has been running a while.
What
a per-frame capture of all 149 engine timers over 25-30 s attributes the spikes. In spike frames (over 60 ms) these stages are far above normal frames:
• _AmStreaming - Sending: 17.7 ms (normally 0.8);
• VoxelServer Update: 31 ms (normally 0.12);
• AEVVRenderer - Load Phase: 15.6 ms (normally 2.1; the headless renderer, B43);
• VPO Update Part 7: 25.7 ms (normally 10.6).
When
continuously while any client is connected, worse the longer it stays connected.
Where
server-side voxel streaming (sending and VoxelServer update) and the headless renderer's load phase.

Measured by connected client

| Client connected | Server spike frames (> 60 ms) | Typical spike | |---|---|---| | Native client, about 4 h, 7.4 GB | 56-66 per 25 s | ~100 ms | | None (8 bots playing) | 0 per 25 s (1,386 frames) | | | Fresh native client, 1-3 min after joining | 23-27 per 25 s | ~76 ms |

Ruled out by A/B

pausing SCAR's 4 Hz re-centring of rig and viewmodel roots changed nothing (56 vs 66 spikes), and parking every bot's viewmodel set far away changed nothing (59).

Physics is not the cost

10 tumbling RigidBody debris chunks cost nothing measurable with no client (55 fps, 56 ms worst). With the old client connected, the same throw dropped the server to 29 fps with 12 frames over 100 ms. Moving voxel objects cost through streaming, not simulation.

Impact

the server's p99 reaches 100-200 ms during play, though the game's own Lua is about 0.5 ms (bots) to 2 ms. Every client's snapshots and hit registration stall with it.

Workaround

restarting a long-running client halves the spikes. SCAR also keeps moving voxel objects to a minimum (debris capped and short-lived) and never streams HD viewmodel sets to non-owners where avoidable.

Suggested fix

budget per-frame streaming work across frames, and look into why a client's streaming cost grows with session length (requests piling up, or cache churn).

F24

HUD root windows turn invisible on a fresh client with no script involved

MediumWhy: observed ↑ index
Who
any player whose HUD is built from script-created NewUI root windows (ED.RootPanel).
What
after a fresh native client joined (9 Oct, ~05:30), the player reported "my UI is missing". On that client UI:GetWindows() listed all of SCAR's HUD windows, but six (Top, Vitals, Weapon, Feed, Tags, Toasts) read visible = false. Nothing in SCAR's scripts sets those six invisible: they are created visible and never toggled. The windows the HUD re-sets every frame (crosshair, markers) were visible. Setting visible = true from a client run-lua brought the panels back at once (confirmed by a client screenshot).
When
on a freshly started native client, after the game had pushed several script updates (each push resets Lua and rebuilds the HUD). The exact trigger is not known.
Where
NewUI root window visibility on the client.

Impact

a HUD that relies on "created visible, never touched" silently loses its panels.

Workaround

SCAR re-reads the six always-on panels 5 times a second and re-shows any that read invisible (ScarHUD.coreRestores counts restores).

Suggested fix

find what clears visible on root windows (window-state restore from a previous instance after a rebuild?), and document which engine events hide script windows.

F25

Client-posed objects render one frame behind the camera

LowWhy: observed↑ index
Who
first-person viewmodels and anything else posed on the client against the camera.
What
SCAR poses its first-person gun and arms (voxel objects, receiveTransform = false) in LateUpdate from the camera object's world transform, after movement and the camera update. When strafing at 30-40 fps the gun visibly trailed the view, by about a frame of movement (10-17 cm, which is large at 0.4 m from the eye), and "hiccuped" with frame-time variation. Turning showed the same lag, hidden by the deliberate sway.
Where
transform propagation from Lua to the renderer for dynamic voxel objects, compared with the camera's own transform.

Workaround

lead the posed position by the camera's last-frame horizontal motion (ScarVM.LEAD).

Suggested fix

apply LateUpdate transforms in the same frame as the camera, or offer a "render relative to camera" flag for viewmodels.

F26

Play clients get pooled parts unparented, and never get empty parent objects

CriticalWhy: measured↑ index
Who
every player of a published world that builds object pools (rigs, held items, prop groups) under empty parent objects.
What
after publishing, players saw invisible bots, guns floating around the map and the placeholder first-person gun. A one-line diagnostic each client reports to the server showed, on the play client, Avatars rigs=0 and VM set=nil (the editor client: 10 rigs, set 1). The server had all 10 "Scar Rig i" roots with their 14 parts, assets bound, and identical object counts to the editor. The play client never received the empty roots ("Scar Rig i", "Scar VM i", "Scar Lobby Props": no component, nothing synced), so Scene:GetObjectByName found nothing, and the parts arrived unparented, drawn at their raw local offsets near the world origin (the "floating guns").
When
on play servers only. In the editor, the client receives the full hierarchy, so the same code works.
Where
object replication to non-editor clients.

Workaround

the server publishes each pool's part ids in a networked field (Object.id is the same on server and clients); clients resolve parts with Scene:GetObjectById, parent them under a client-only stand-in root and pose them. Prop groups became top-level objects with world transforms. Verified by forcing the id path on an editor client: 10 rigs resolved by id, torsos on their bots, first-person set active.

Suggested fix

replicate the hierarchy (including empty parents) to play clients the same way as to editor clients, or document loudly that play clients receive children unparented, and give the editor a "preview as play client" mode.
